---
title: "Schedules"
description: "The three ways to schedule a job in Cronch — interval, cron and once — and how catch-up handles runs missed while your machine was off."
---

Every rule has one schedule, chosen from three modes. Pick the mode that matches how you think about the job.

## Interval

Run every *N* seconds, minutes, hours or days. The simplest option — "every 15 minutes", "every 6 hours" — with no cron syntax to remember. Set the number and the unit, and Cronch counts from when the rule starts. The rhythm is kept: an hourly rule that started at 10:17 keeps running at a quarter past, even after the computer was off for a while.

## Cron

For calendar-based timing — "at 9:00 every weekday", "on the 1st of the month" — use a cron expression. Cronch follows standard cron:

- **5 fields** — `minute hour day-of-month month day-of-week`; seconds are assumed to be `0`.
- **6 fields** — adds a leading seconds field for finer timing.
- **Day of week** — `0`–`7`, where both `0` and `7` are Sunday (`1` is Monday), or names like `MON-FRI`.
- **Day of month and day of week together** — a day that matches *either* one fires, just like classic cron.

| Expression | Runs |
| --- | --- |
| `0 9 * * *` | Every day at 09:00 |
| `0 9 * * 1-5` | Weekdays (Monday–Friday) at 09:00 |
| `*/15 * * * *` | Every 15 minutes |
| `0 0 1 * *` | At midnight on the 1st of every month |
| `30 0 9 * * MON` | Mondays at 09:00:30 (6 fields, with seconds) |

Expressions are evaluated in your local time, including daylight-saving changes.

<Tip>
    New to cron? Start from the example in the field (`0 9 * * *`) and change one part at a time. Interval mode is often easier for "every so often" jobs; cron shines for "at this clock time".
</Tip>

## Once

Run a single time at a specific date and time — `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, in your local time. The time must be in the future when you save the rule. Useful for a one-off reminder script or a delayed task. After it runs, the rule is done.

## Catch-up: runs missed while the machine was off or asleep

Computers sleep and shut down, so a scheduled time can pass while Cronch can't run anything. A run that is more than **five minutes late** counts as **missed** — whether Cronch wasn't running, the computer was asleep, or everything was paused. (A run that is only slightly late, for example because the system was busy, simply runs.)

Each rule has a **Catch up if missed** option (on by default) that decides what happens to a missed run:

- **On** — as soon as Cronch can (when it starts, the computer wakes, or you resume), the rule runs **once**, right away. Multiple missed occurrences are **coalesced** into that single catch-up run, so a weekend of hourly jobs doesn't fire dozens of times at once.
- **Off** — the missed occurrences are skipped; the rule simply waits for its next scheduled time.

A missed **Once** schedule follows the same option: with catch-up on it runs as soon as Cronch can; with catch-up off its moment has passed, and the rule is marked **Expired**.

Catch-up runs are labelled **catch-up** in the rule's [history](/cronch/history).

<Note>
    Cronch re-checks its schedule continuously, so clock changes and sleep/wake are handled without relying on operating-system power events. You don't need to configure anything for this.
</Note>
