Every rule has one schedule, chosen from three modes. Pick the mode that matches how you think about the job.

| If the job should run… | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every so often, at no particular clock time | **Interval** | Every 15 minutes, every 6 hours |
| At a clock time or on calendar days | **Cron** | 09:00 on weekdays, midnight on the 1st |
| Exactly once | **Once** | 2026-12-24 18:00 |

## Interval

Run every *N* **seconds, minutes, hours or days** — "every 15 minutes", "every 6 hours" — with no syntax to remember. The number can be from 1 to 1,000,000, and the whole interval from 1 second up to 100 years. New rules start as **every 5 minutes**.

- **The first run is one interval after you save**, not immediately. Save an hourly rule at 10:17 and it first runs at 11:17. Use **Run now** if you want a run straight away.
- **The rhythm is kept.** An hourly rule that started at 10:17 keeps running at a quarter past — across restarts, sleep and time with Cronch closed.
- **Editing re-anchors it.** When you change the interval, or switch the rule off and on again, the rhythm restarts from the start of the rule's last run.
- **Intervals are exact durations**, not clock times. A daily interval that runs at 09:00 runs at 10:00 or 08:00 after a daylight-saving change. If the clock time matters, use **Cron**.

## Cron

For calendar-based timing — "at 9:00 every weekday", "on the 1st of the month" — use a cron expression. New rules suggest `0 9 * * *` (every day at 09:00).

| Expression | Runs |
| --- | --- |
| `0 9 * * *` | Every day at 09:00 |
| `0 9 * * 1-5` | Weekdays (Monday–Friday) at 09:00 |
| `*/15 * * * *` | Every 15 minutes |
| `0 0 1 * *` | At midnight on the 1st of every month |
| `0 18 * * 5L` | 18:00 on the last Friday of every month |
| `30 0 9 * * MON` | Mondays at 09:00:30 (6 fields, with seconds) |

Cron expressions are evaluated in your computer's **local time** and follow it through daylight-saving changes: `0 9 * * *` stays at 09:00 all year. The full syntax — fields, names, `L`, `W`, `#`, shortcuts like `@daily`, and the exact daylight-saving rules — is in [Cron syntax](/cronch/cron-syntax).

## Once

Run a single time at a date and time, typed as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` in your local time.

- The time must be **in the future** when you save. (Editing other fields of an existing Once rule keeps its original time.)
- A time that doesn't exist or happens twice on a daylight-saving day — inside the hour the clock skips or repeats — is rejected; pick a time outside that hour.
- After it runs, the rule's **next** shows **—**: it's done, and it stays in the list until you delete it.
- **Run now** before its time doesn't use it up: the rule still runs at its time.
- The moment is stored as an exact instant. If you travel to another time zone, it still runs at the same instant, which shows as a different local time.

If the computer is off or asleep at that moment, what happens depends on **Catch up if missed** — see [Missed runs, sleep & pausing](/cronch/catch-up).

<Tip>
    New to cron? Start from the suggestion in the field and change one part at a time. Interval mode is often easier for "every so often" jobs; cron shines for "at this clock time".
</Tip>
