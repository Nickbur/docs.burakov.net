Sidedrop's privacy comes from how it's built: your files travel directly between two browsers, so there's
no copy of them on any server.

## No account, no analytics

There's no sign-up and no login, and Sidedrop includes no analytics or advertising trackers.

## What the signaling server sees

The signaling server introduces the two browsers. To do that it sees:

- each device's **IP address** (from its connection),
- the **room** and its **code**, and
- the connection-setup messages the browsers exchange — which include their **network addresses** and the
    fingerprints used to set up encryption.

It **never** receives your files, their names or their sizes.

It keeps only the room — its code and the two devices' anonymous connection tokens — for up to 2 hours,
and deletes it about 45 seconds after both devices leave. Like most web servers, it also writes standard
request logs, which include IP addresses.

## Other services

| Service | Sees |
| --- | --- |
| **Google's public STUN servers** | Each device's IP address — they help the browsers discover how to reach each other. |
| **A relay (TURN) server**, when one is needed | Both devices' IP addresses and how much encrypted data passes — never what's inside. |
| **Google Fonts** | Your IP address when the page loads its font. |

## One thing to know about trust

The fingerprints that secure the direct connection are exchanged through the signaling server, and
Sidedrop doesn't offer a way to compare them yourself. So you rely on that server not to interfere with
the handshake. Your files are still never sent through it.

## What stays in your browser

- Files you receive are written to your browser's own site storage until you save them. Files you never
    saved — and canceled or failed transfers — can **stay there after you close the tab**; clear the site's
    data in your browser settings to remove them.
- Your language and theme choice are remembered in the browser.
- Nothing else is kept.
