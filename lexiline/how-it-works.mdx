Lexiline turns a topic into a standing request: *tell me when something relevant appears.* Here's what
happens between following a topic and seeing a match.

## Searching on a schedule

Lexiline's servers search the news and the web for each topic's **title** — whether or not your phone is
on. A new topic is searched immediately — up to 20 results from the last **7 days** on Free, or from up
to the last **90 days** on Pro — and after that each search looks at the latest 15 results.

How often a topic is checked depends on your plan:

| Plan | How often |
| --- | --- |
| **Free** | **Once a day** |
| **Pro** | **Adapts to how busy the topic is** — anywhere from about **once an hour** to **once a day**. A check that finds new matches brings the next one sooner; quiet checks push it later, so a topic with no news settles at about once a day. Topics with **Notify me on every match** on are checked close to real time. |

## Reading for meaning

Each result is scored by an AI against your topic — its title, **What to watch for**, and your
**Include** / **Exclude** keywords — and against a few matches you recently liked or rejected. Results
scoring at or above the topic's **Match strictness** go into your feed, each with a one-line reason.
Sites you've muted are skipped before scoring.

The same article is never added twice to a topic, even if it turns up under slightly different links.

## Nothing until there's something

A brand-new or quiet topic can sit empty for a while — Lexiline only adds what genuinely matches. An
empty topic isn't broken; it's waiting. If it stays empty for days, see
[Sharpen a topic](/lexiline/tuning-topics).

<Note>
    The time shown on a match is when Lexiline **found** it. On Free, and for quiet topics on Pro, checks
    run about once a day, so a match can appear up to a day after it was published.
</Note>
