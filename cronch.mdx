Cronch is a small desktop app that runs commands and scripts on a schedule, from your **system tray**, on **Windows and macOS**. Add a rule, pick a schedule and a shell, flip it on — Cronch runs it in the background and keeps a full history of every run. There's no account and no cloud: everything stays on your machine.

## How it works

<Steps>
    <Step title="Cronch lives in your tray">
        It starts at login and sits in the system tray (Windows) or menu bar (macOS). Closing the window only hides it — Cronch keeps running and keeps your schedules.
    </Step>
    <Step title="You write rules">
        A rule is one command, a shell to run it in, and a schedule: every *N* minutes, a cron expression, or a single date and time.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Cronch runs them on time">
        When a rule comes due, Cronch starts the command in the background, captures its output and records whether it worked. Runs missed while the computer was asleep or off can catch up once when it's back.
    </Step>
    <Step title="You check the history">
        Every run's status, exit code and output stay on your machine for as long as you choose, and a run that fails or times out shows a system notification.
    </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
    Cronch is a **tray-resident** app: your jobs fire while it's running and you're signed in. That's why it launches at login by default — so your schedules keep running across restarts. You can turn that off in [Settings](/cronch/settings).
</Note>

## What people schedule with it

Nightly backups, periodic sync or upload scripts, a morning report, cleanup tasks — anything you'd otherwise wire into `cron`, `launchd` or Task Scheduler.
