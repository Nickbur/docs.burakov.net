Rowbots works like a responsible senior engineer with a QA team behind them. It doesn't reach for code
first: it plans the whole change, builds it, proves it against reality and only then asks for your
signature. You stand **at the door, not in the loop**.

## The life of a work item

<Steps>
    <Step title="Intake & triage">
        Everything you hand it lands in one inbox. Rowbots deduplicates it, interprets it and ranks it
        against the priorities in your [constitution](/rowbots/projects-and-fleet#the-constitution).
    </Step>
    <Step title="Scheduling">
        An item starts only when it is worth its cost, fits the [monthly budget](/rowbots/budget) and
        doesn't collide with other work in flight. Items that touch different parts of the code run in
        parallel; overlapping ones are ordered.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Planning">
        Rowbots estimates, analyses risks, scouts the libraries and APIs it is unsure about, and writes a
        complete plan with a cost forecast before any code changes. Larger work is split into a set of
        small, dependent steps — each one becomes its own pull request.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Building">
        The executor writes the code and its own tests inside an isolated
        [sandbox](/rowbots/security-and-privacy#sandboxing), using your build and test commands.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Judging">
        An independent judge checks the result on a fresh copy of the exact commit, with its own hidden
        acceptance tests and the full check set. A failure goes back to the executor to fix, and the judge
        verifies the fix again. See [Verification](/rowbots/verification).
    </Step>
    <Step title="Pull request">
        The verified change is opened as a pull request in your own git host, with the evidence, the
        decisions made along the way and the expected impact.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Your merge">
        You merge — one by one, or a batch of small, safe changes at once. Your own CI/CD deploys it;
        Rowbots never touches production.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Watching the result">
        After a merge, Rowbots can watch the signals you've connected (errors, reviews, analytics) for a
        while. If the change made things worse, it opens an urgent revert pull request.
    </Step>
</Steps>

## Where you step in

You touch exactly two points:

1. **Answering an escalation.** When a decision is about *what* the product should be — a value or a
   priority your constitution doesn't cover yet — Rowbots asks you once. It never blocks while waiting:
   it parks that part and keeps working on everything else. Your answer is written into the constitution,
   so the same question is never asked twice.
2. **Merging.** Merge is the final acceptance gate and always stays with you.

Everything else — how to implement, which library to use, how to route around an obstacle — Rowbots
decides on its own, as long as the change can be undone. When something can't be undone, autonomy pauses:
Rowbots prefers to make it reversible (feature flags, migrations that expand before they contract) or asks
you first.

## Trust modes

For each task you choose how much Rowbots confirms before acting:

| Mode | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Confirm** — "confirm my understanding" | Rowbots shows you every plan and waits for your OK before building. |
| **Usual** — "do it like we usually do" | Rowbots follows the decisions already made across your fleet and asks only about goals, taste, money and irreversible steps. |
| **Trust** — "I trust you, proceed" | Rowbots decides more on its own; irreversible steps still need you. |

## Your attention is budgeted too

On a fleet, your attention is the real bottleneck. Rowbots ranks everything that needs you by risk and
confidence: trivial, safe changes arrive as a quick batch to approve, high-stakes ones get a focused,
individual review. A daily attention budget (30 minutes by default) caps how much it asks of you; the
rest waits without blocking anything. As your constitution matures, the number of things that need you
keeps shrinking.

## When something goes wrong

- **An interruption mid-work** resumes from the last checkpoint or rolls back cleanly — no half-finished
  state is left in your repositories.
- **A defect that slips past the judge and your review** becomes a permanent check, so the same class of
  bug is caught next time.
- **A pattern of failures across several products** stops autonomous work across the fleet and asks you
  before anything continues.
