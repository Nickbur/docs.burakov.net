## One inbox for everything

Hand Rowbots whatever you have, in your own words and language:

- **Tasks** — "add CSV export to the reports page".
- **Ideas** — half-formed is fine; Rowbots turns them into a plan or a proposal.
- **Feedback and bug reports** — from you, your team or your users.
- **Signals** — errors, store reviews, support messages, uptime alerts.

You and your team add work directly in the workspace inbox. Further channels plug into the same inbox:

| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| Owner & team | the dashboard inbox (text, screenshots, files), a per-workspace email address, a Telegram bot, issues labelled `rowbots` |
| Errors & crashes | Sentry, a generic webhook |
| Store reviews | Google Play, App Store Connect |
| Support | forwarded email from any help desk, a generic webhook |
| Product metrics & uptime | PostHog, Uptime Kuma, Better Stack, a generic webhook |

<Note>
    During early access the inbox is open first; the external channel connectors are being enabled step
    by step.
</Note>

## Triage

Every item is deduplicated, interpreted and ranked against your
[constitution](/rowbots/projects-and-fleet#the-constitution) into one backlog. Ranking by the priorities
you set is automatic; changing those priorities is your call.

## Rowbots pushes back

A senior who never pushes back isn't helping. Your own requests are triaged too:

- **When something conflicts with your priorities**, Rowbots says so, with reasoning — for example, "this
  takes 60% of the month's budget; your constitution says the other project matters more — confirm or
  override?"
- **A subjective bad bet** gets strong pushback and a recorded forecast. You can override it by
  explicitly acknowledging the warning.
- **Something objectively broken** — harmful, incoherent, or a violation of a confirmed invariant — is
  refused outright. You can't override reality.

The rule is simple: **Rowbots refuses what is broken, not what is unusual.**

## Input from strangers is handled safely

Reviews, support messages and error reports often contain text written by people you don't know. That
text is read only by a triage step with **no tools, no code and no secrets**, which turns it into a
structured summary. Raw outside text never reaches the agents that write code.

Outside input can start only **maintenance-level work** — a bug, a security issue, a performance
problem. A feature request from the public becomes a **proposal** for you to accept or decline.

## When nothing is queued

With no tasks waiting, Rowbots keeps your existing products healthy. It works through a fleet-wide list
of maintenance work in this order:

1. security
2. correctness and bugs
3. cost savings
4. technical debt and performance
5. test coverage
6. polish

Each item has an estimated value and cost. Rowbots works top-down only **while the value is worth the
cost and the budget allows** — below that line it genuinely stops. Maintenance never adds features or
changes product direction; that always takes a task from you.

<Tip>
    Always-on maintenance is part of the paid plans. On Free, Rowbots works only on the tasks you give it.
</Tip>
