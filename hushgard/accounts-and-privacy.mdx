## One account, every device

Hushgard uses **passwordless sign-in**: your email and a one-time code (or your **Google** account, or **Sign in with Apple** on iPhone) — or your company account, if your organization uses [single sign-on](/hushgard/sso). The same account carries your identity across web, desktop, iOS and Android, and it's the same account used across the other apps at burakov.net. Manage it under [Account & settings](/hushgard/account-and-settings).

Notifications follow your device's language; emails are sent in English or Russian.

## What stays on your organization's server

Your **content** — message text, file contents and call media — is end-to-end encrypted and lives on your organization's own server (its node), in the place it chose. The server stores it encrypted.

Exceptions, all labelled in the app: **public topics** (stored as they are so your organization can search them), **webhook messages** (seen by the server as they arrive, then stored encrypted), and chats and topics the **[AI assistant](/hushgard/ai-assistant)** has been added to, whose content the assistant reads on that server.

## What the control plane sees

Identity, push and licensing are brokered by Hushgard's shared control plane so accounts work everywhere. It holds:

- **Your account** and your sign-in sessions.
- **Organization membership** — which organizations you belong to and your role — and each organization's plan, license and billing.
- **Each organization server's address**, version and last check-in.
- **Public encryption keys** — the directory that lets others encrypt to you, its history, and the contacts you've verified — and, if you choose to keep one there, your passphrase-sealed key backup, which it can't open.
- **High-security device certificates** for your devices.
- **Delivery metadata** needed to route pushes and calls, and invitations to chats in other organizations (who invited whom — never the chat's name).
- **Single sign-on and provisioning**, when your organization uses them: its identity-provider settings, verified domains, and the people and groups the provider provisions. See [Single sign-on](/hushgard/sso).
- **Meeting invitations to guests**, when your organization sends them by e-mail: the guests' addresses (deleted after the meeting), the time, the join link and — only if the organizer includes it — the title. Never the agenda.

It does **not** hold your message content or call media. We describe this honestly as **data residency**, not "no server in the middle": your content stays on your organization's server, while identity is centrally brokered. Hushgard's own platform administration can see that an organization exists, its plan and its server's health — never its messages.

## What metadata is visible

End-to-end encryption protects **what** you say — not the fact that a conversation is happening:

- Your organization's **server** can see the "envelope": who is talking to whom, group membership, timestamps and who is online. That's inherent to delivering messages — and on a self-hosted node, it's *your* server.
- For scheduling, the server also knows **when** things are due — not what they are: the start time of a [meeting](/hushgard/chat#meetings) and who answered *going*, when a [scheduled message](/hushgard/chat#send-later-and-reminders) is sent (it waits encrypted until then) and when a reminder fires. Your **status**, **do not disturb**, **working hours** and **out of office** are stored on it too, so it can hold notifications and show them to the people you work with.
- **Message sizes are padded** into fixed buckets before encryption, so an observer of encrypted traffic learns much less from length.
- **Link previews are built on the sender's device** and travel inside the encrypted message, so neither the server nor the people reading fetch the link — and Hushgard has **no phone-contact sync**. Those are two common ways other apps quietly leak the URLs you share and your social graph.
- **Notifications are generic by default:** a push tells your device a message arrived, carrying only an opaque reference — not who sent it or which chat — and the app fills in the details after it opens. Call pushes ring without revealing the caller to the notification service. A self-hosted organization can choose named notifications on its server (`MESSAGE_PUSH_HIDE_METADATA`).

The principle: **data that isn't collected can't be handed over or lost.**

## Metadata-minimal mode

For organizations with a stricter threat model, an admin can turn on **metadata-minimal mode** (see [Organization settings](/hushgard/organization-settings#metadata-minimal-mode)). The server then stops emitting peer-visible metadata: **no typing or recording indicators**, **no delivery or read receipts**, **no online status or last seen**, **no link previews**, no "opened" status on [view-once](/hushgard/chat#view-once) items, no "listened" status on [voice messages](/hushgard/chat#voice-messages), and notifications are forced to their most generic form. It's enforced on the server, not just hidden in the app.

## Your data rights

From **Account → Account settings** in the app:

- **Export your account data** — your account, sessions and subscriptions, as a file.
- **Export your data from organization servers** — one file covering every organization server you use: your chats, saved messages, reports, and your topic memberships, posts, comments, reactions and votes. Encrypted messages are exported **as stored — encrypted**.
- **Erase your data on servers** — choose the organization servers. Your messages, posts and comments are replaced with "deleted" for others; your drafts, reactions, votes, bookmarks, folders, invitations and all your chat and topic memberships are deleted. This works even when an organization is read-only.
- **Delete your account** — your data on the organization servers is erased first, then the account itself. You can restore the account within **30 days** from the link we email you or by signing in again; erased server data can't be restored.

Organization admins can also set a **retention policy** so chat messages are deleted automatically after a chosen period (see [Organization settings](/hushgard/organization-settings#retention)). Managed instances come with a data-processing agreement in the [Terms](https://burakov.net/projects/hushgard/terms).

## Verifying contacts

Because encryption only helps if you're talking to the right person, every contact has a **safety number** you can compare or scan. See [Security & encryption](/hushgard/security).
