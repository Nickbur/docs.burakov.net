A dead man's switch is only as trustworthy as its promise to still be running years from now.
So the core of Quietletter is **open-source and self-hostable** — what you set up doesn't depend
on any one company staying alive.

## The open-source core

The self-hostable server (`quietletter-core`) is released under the **AGPL-3.0** license — the same
model trusted privacy tools like Bitwarden and Standard Notes use. You can read every line,
run it yourself, and keep it running on your own terms.

<Card title="What the core gives you" icon="server">
    A standalone server you run with `docker compose up`: your switches, check-ins, the firing
    engine, and the recipient release page — all on infrastructure you control. It stays
    **content-blind**, exactly like the hosted service.
</Card>

## Quick start

<Steps>
    <Step title="Get the core">
        Clone [the repository](https://github.com/Nickbur/quietletter_core) and copy the production
        settings file `env/env.prod` to `.env`:

        ```bash
        git clone https://github.com/Nickbur/quietletter_core.git
        cd quietletter_core
        cp env/env.prod .env
        ```
    </Step>
    <Step title="Fill in your settings">
        Generate a strong token (`openssl rand -hex 32`) and set `OWNER_TOKEN` — it's how your app
        authenticates to your instance. Then replace the remaining `REPLACE_ME` values: your
        instance's public URL, your email for check-in warnings, and your SMTP details (or clear
        `OWNER_EMAIL` and the `SMTP_*` values to keep mail in the console). The server won't start
        until the token, the public URL and the email are set.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Run it">
        `docker compose up --build` starts PostgreSQL and the server, applies the database
        migrations, and begins the check-in sweep. The server listens on `http://localhost:8080`.
        Point your client at your instance's URL and you're live.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Connect the app">
        In the app, open **Settings → Self-hosted instance** (or tap **Connect your instance** on
        the sign-in screen) and enter your instance's URL and `OWNER_TOKEN`. The app reloads
        against your instance — no account needed.
    </Step>
</Steps>

Configuration, email delivery, backups, running without Docker and the full API are covered in
[Running your instance](/quietletter/running-your-instance).

<Note>
    While the app is connected to your instance, every feature is unlocked and there's no
    subscription. Check-in reminders keep firing on your device, but push notifications from the
    hosted service are off.
</Note>

## Move your switches

Your switches move between the hosted service and your own instance — in either direction — as a
single file.

<Steps>
    <Step title="Export">
        While the app is connected to where your switches live now, open **Settings → Backup &
        transfer → Export switches** and confirm with your PIN (or Face ID / fingerprint). On the
        web the file downloads; on iOS and Android the share sheet opens so you can save or send it.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Switch sides">
        Connect your instance in **Settings → Self-hosted instance** — or, to come back, choose
        **Use the hosted service** there and sign in.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Import">
        Open **Settings → Backup & transfer → Import switches**, pick the file and confirm. You get
        a report of what was imported and what was skipped, and why.
    </Step>
</Steps>

What the file holds, and how an import behaves:

- **Encrypted stays encrypted.** Zero-knowledge messages, files and secrets travel as ciphertext
    with their passphrase-wrapped key. After the move your recipients open them with the same
    passphrase, and no server ever sees it.
- **The rest is plain text.** Titles, recipient emails and phone numbers, check-in settings and
    any message you marked readable are readable in the file. Keep it somewhere safe.
- **Nothing fires early.** Every imported switch starts its check-in timer at the moment of
    import, and a switch you had turned off stays off.
- **Safe to repeat.** A switch that's already there is skipped — never duplicated or overwritten —
    so importing the same file twice changes nothing.
- **Live switches only.** Switches that have already fired are not exported.
- **Hosted-service rules apply.** An import into the hosted service follows your plan (one switch
    on the free plan; files, secrets, private pages and SMS warnings need Pro), and a switch with a
    public page is skipped, because the hosted service doesn't publish public pages.

## Continuity guarantees

- **Export anytime.** You can always export every switch to one file and import it into your own
    instance — or back — so your setup is never trapped inside one service.
- **Published shutdown protocol.** If the hosted service ever winds down, you get long advance
    notice, a final export, and a migration guide to self-hosting.
- **Honest limits.** "It outlives any company" is only partly solvable — self-hosting is the
    real backstop, and we document that plainly rather than overclaiming.

<Note>
    The truly-public "make it public to the world" action is available **only on self-hosted
    instances** — the hosted service deliberately doesn't publish content to the open web.
</Note>
