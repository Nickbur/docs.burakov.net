## One account, every device

Bastion uses **passwordless sign-in**: enter your email address and a one-time code, or sign in with Google (or Apple on iOS). The same account carries your identity across web, desktop, iOS and Android — and it's the same unified account used across the wider family of apps, so there's no separate signup per app. From **Account** you can change your name, username, picture and email address, and see and sign out your other sessions.

The apps are available in **English, Russian, German, French, Spanish, Italian and Dutch**, and your choice is saved with your account. Notifications follow your device's language; emails are sent in English or Russian.

## What stays on your server

Your **content** — message text, file contents and call media — is end-to-end encrypted and, on a self-hosted node, lives on infrastructure you chose, in the region you chose. It never leaves your box in a readable form; the server only ever holds ciphertext.

## What the control plane sees

Identity, push and licensing are brokered by a shared control plane so accounts work everywhere. That means it handles:

- **Sign-in** (verifying your email and issuing your session).
- **Organization membership** (which organizations you belong to and your role) and **licensing** (which plan an organization is on).
- **Public encryption keys** — the directory that lets others encrypt to you, the contacts you've verified and, if you choose to keep one there, your passphrase-sealed key backup, which it can't open.
- **Delivery metadata** needed to route pushes and calls, and invitations to chats in other organizations (who invited whom — never the chat's name).

It does **not** hold your message content or call media — those are encrypted end to end. We describe this honestly as **data residency**, not "no server in the middle": your content stays on your node, while identity is centrally brokered.

## What metadata is visible

End-to-end encryption protects **what** you say — not the fact that a conversation is happening. Being honest about that:

- Your **node** (your own server, or your managed instance) can see the "envelope": who is talking to whom, group membership, timestamps and who is online. That's inherent to delivering messages — and on a self-hosted node it's *your* server.
- **Message sizes are padded** into fixed buckets before encryption, so an observer of encrypted traffic learns much less from length.
- Bastion has **no link-preview/unfurl** feature and **no phone-contact sync** — two common ways other apps quietly leak the URLs you share and your social graph.
- **Notifications are private by default:** a push tells your device a message arrived, carrying only an opaque reference — **not** who sent it or which chat — and the app fills in the details after it opens. Call pushes ring without revealing the caller to the notification service. An organization can opt into richer (named) notifications if it prefers.

The principle: **data that isn't collected can't be handed over or lost.**

## Metadata-minimal mode

For organizations with a stricter threat model, an admin can turn on **metadata-minimal mode** (see [Administration](/bastion/administration)). When it's on, the node stops emitting peer-visible metadata: **no typing indicators**, **no delivery or read receipts**, **no online status or last seen**, and notifications are forced to their most generic form. It's enforced on the server, not just hidden in the app.

## Your data rights

From **Account → Account settings** in the app:

- **Export your account data** — your account, sessions and subscriptions, as a file.
- **Export your data from organization servers** — every organization server you use, in one file: your chats, saved messages, reports, and your topic memberships, posts, comments, reactions and votes.
- **Erase your data on servers** — choose the organization servers; your messages, posts and comments are removed (shown as deleted to others) and everything else of yours is deleted.
- **Delete your account** — your data on the organization servers is erased first, then the account itself. The account can be restored within 30 days from the link we email you; erased server data can't be.

Organization admins can also set a **retention policy** so content is auto-deleted after a chosen period (see [Administration](/bastion/administration)). Managed instances come with a data-processing agreement in the [Terms](https://bastion.burakov.net/terms/).

## Verifying contacts

Because encryption only helps if you're talking to the right person, every contact has a **safety number** you can compare or scan. See [Security](/bastion/security).
