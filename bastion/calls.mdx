Bastion has **end-to-end encrypted audio and video calls** built in — one-to-one or as a group, with screen sharing on every device. The media runs through a server that lives inside your own node, so the call itself never travels through anyone else's cloud — and if a call can't be encrypted, Bastion doesn't connect it.

## Starting a call

Open a chat and tap the **call** or **video** button, or tap **New call** on the **Calls** tab and pick people — Bastion opens the chat with them, or creates one. Calling a direct chat or a group rings everyone in it (bots and the AI assistant are never rung). If a call is already going on in that chat, you join it instead of ringing everyone again — a banner in the chat offers **Join** too.

**Channels** don't ring the whole room: starting a call posts a **Join call** banner in the channel and rings only the people you @-mention.

A call holds up to **50 people with video**, or **100** when it's audio only.

<Note>
    If your microphone or camera isn't available, the call still connects — you join muted or with the camera off, rather than failing to connect at all.
</Note>

## During a call

**Sound**

- **Mute**, and see your own microphone level. If you start talking while muted, Bastion tells you.
- **Noise suppression** removes keyboard clicks, traffic and background chatter on your own device. It's on by default and you can switch it off. Nothing is sent to an outside service for it.
- **Pick devices** — microphone, camera and speakers. On phones, switch between earpiece, loudspeaker and Bluetooth; hold the phone to your ear and the screen turns off.
- **Low-data mode** pauses incoming video on a weak connection, or whenever you choose, and keeps the audio going.

**Video**

- Turn the **camera** on or off (an audio call can become a video call) and **flip** between front and back cameras.
- **Blur your background** or replace it with a picture — including backgrounds your organization provides. It's done on your device before encryption.
- **Layouts:** speaker view or grid; in a one-to-one call the other person fills the screen and your own preview can be dragged anywhere. **Pin** someone with a long press (or `F`); when a group call drops to two people, it switches to the one-to-one view. You can hide or mirror your own preview.
- **Picture-in-picture:** leave the call screen and the video keeps going in a small window you can drag; on phones it continues in the system's picture-in-picture when you switch apps.

**Screen sharing**

- **Share your screen** from phones, the web app and the desktop app. On a computer you can share a single window or tab, **with its sound**.
- While you share, a banner says so and your own preview is hidden, so you don't get an endless mirror. Viewers can go full screen and zoom in.

**People and conversation**

- **Add people:** ring chat members who aren't in the call yet, or add someone to the chat and ring them in one step.
- Everyone's status is visible: invited, ringing, joining, connected, on hold, reconnecting, declined, no answer, left.
- Send **reactions** and **raise your hand**. Open the **chat** beside the call to keep writing.
- **Hold** a call — the others see that you're on hold — and switch between two calls: hold one, answer the other.
- **Hosts** can mute a participant, remove someone, lock the call so nobody else can join, and mute everyone who joins a large call.

**Keep using the app:** leave the call screen and a small bar stays on top of every page with mute and hang-up; tap it to go back.

The header shows the call's state — ringing, the running timer, *Restoring your connection…* after a network drop — and each participant's connection quality. A lock badge confirms your media is encrypted, and shows **verified** when the people in the call have verified keys. **Call diagnostics** shows codec, bitrate, packet loss and delay; it stays on your device.

## Guests by link

When your organization allows it, a member can invite people **without a Bastion account**:

1. Create a **call link** — with an expiry, a limit on how many times it can be used and, if you like, a passcode.
2. The guest opens the link in a browser and enters a name.
3. A host lets them in from the **lobby**. Guests always wait there first.

Guest calls are **end-to-end encrypted** too. The secret that unlocks the call is in the part of the link that browsers never send to a server, so not even your organization's server can listen in. A guest sees only the call — never the chat's history — gets a **Guest** badge, and can use a short in-call chat that isn't stored anywhere.

Guests are off until an admin switches them on — see [Organization settings](/bastion/organization-settings#calls).

## Recording

When your organization allows it, a participant can **record** a call. The recording is made on that person's device — your server only ever sees encrypted media, so it couldn't record if it tried. Everyone in the call sees a **Recording** indicator, and anyone who joins later is told. The finished recording is saved to the chat as an encrypted file, never left unencrypted on a server.

Recording is off until an admin switches it on, and is never available in high-security chats.

## Live captions and notes

With the [AI assistant](/bastion/ai-assistant) module, the assistant can join calls in chats where it's switched on: it shows **live captions** and, when the call ends, posts a **summary** to the chat. It appears as its own tile marked **AI**, and you can turn it off for a call. It never joins calls in direct or high-security chats. Speech is recognized on your organization's own server.

## Incoming calls that actually ring

A call is only useful if you notice it. Bastion rings you like a real phone:

<Steps>
    <Step title="On your phone">
        Incoming Bastion calls use the native system call screen on iOS and Android — they ring on the lock screen, before you've opened the app. Every phone you're signed in on rings — being online on a computer, or having Bastion open on another phone, doesn't stop it. Answer on one device and the others stop ringing.
    </Step>
    <Step title="On web and desktop">
        A full-screen ringer with accept and decline does the same job in the browser and the desktop app, with a system notification when the browser tab is in the background. A second call while you're ringing waits in line; during a call it shows as **call waiting** — put the current call on hold and take it, or decline it.
        <Note>
            When none of your phones is online, an incoming call also reaches your browser as a system notification — even with no Bastion tab open — once you've allowed notifications for Bastion. The desktop app rings while it's running.
        </Note>
    </Step>
    <Step title="Answer or decline">
        Accept to join the call; decline to send it away. If the caller cancels first, the ring dismisses itself. Unanswered calls stop ringing after 45 seconds.
    </Step>
</Steps>

## Missed calls and history

The **Calls** tab has **New call** to start one, and **Recent** calls — including chats hosted by other organizations you take part in — marked **In progress**, **Answered**, **Missed**, **Declined** or **No answer**, with a one-tap **call back** and a badge for calls you missed. A chat's info page lists that chat's recent calls with their duration. You can delete a call from your own history; how long history is kept follows your organization's retention setting.

When a call ends unexpectedly, Bastion says why — for example the other person was busy or moved the call to another device, your browser can't encrypt the call (update it, or use the app), or a high-security chat's key changed (call again). If your connection drops, the call reconnects by itself, and a hang-up made while offline still ends the call once you're back.

## What's encrypted

Audio, video, screen sharing, reactions and the in-call chat are encrypted end to end between participants. Each call gets **its own key, replaced every time someone joins or leaves**: a newcomer can't decrypt what was said before they arrived, and someone who left can't hear what follows. The keys are delivered only to the [verified keys](/bastion/security#verify-who-youre-talking-to) of the people in the call, so the server can't slip in a device of its own. The media server that carries the streams runs on your node, and if a device can't establish end-to-end encryption for the call, Bastion **won't connect it** rather than fall back to unencrypted media.

**High-security chats** can be called too. Their call key comes from the chat's current high-security key, so every participant's device must be up to date with the chat's membership; if the membership changes during the call, the call ends and can be started again with the new key.

Bastion has **no phone-line dial-in**: a call over the telephone network can't be end-to-end encrypted.

<Note>
    On a self-hosted node, calls need the bundled media server to be running and reachable — see [Self-hosting](/bastion/self-hosting).
</Note>
