Bastion is **end-to-end encrypted by default**. There's no toggle to forget: every message, file and call is sealed on the sender's device and opened only on the recipients'. The server — even one you run yourself — only ever handles ciphertext.

## The building blocks

Bastion's encryption is built on **vetted, widely-audited cryptography** (X25519 key exchange, XChaCha20-Poly1305 for content, Argon2id for your passphrase). You don't have to think about any of that day to day — it's the foundation the features below stand on.

## Verify who you're talking to

End-to-end encryption protects you from everyone except an attacker who can quietly swap keys in transit. To close that gap, every conversation has a **safety number** both sides can compare:

<Steps>
    <Step title="Open the chat info">
        From a chat's header, open its info page and find a member's safety number.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Compare it">
        Compare the number in person, over a call, or through another trusted channel.
    </Step>
    <Step title="You're verified">
        If they match, no one is in the middle. If they don't, stop and investigate.
    </Step>
</Steps>

## Your key, your backup

Your identity key lives on **your device**. From the **Security** screen you can:

- **Back it up** with a passphrase you choose, and download or copy the backup.
- **Restore it** on a new phone, laptop or browser — same account, same history, across every device you use.

The Security screen shows whether your current key is backed up. If it isn't, signing out warns you first and offers to back it up — signing out removes the key from that device.

<Warning>
    The backup is protected by your passphrase and the server never has it. If you lose both the passphrase and every signed-in device, that key can't be recovered — by design. Keep the backup somewhere safe.
</Warning>

### Lost your key? Start over

No backup and no signed-in device left? On the **Security** screen, choose **Start over with a new key**. Your account gets a new key, and your conversations come back on their own: as soon as another member of a chat or topic is online, their app hands the conversation to your new key — history included. Until then, the chat shows that it's waiting for access, and messages you can't open yet are marked as such.

<Note>
    Starting over can't recover anything only your old key could open — for example, a conversation where no one else still has access. Your old backup won't work with the new key, so back up the new one.
</Note>

## What the server can and can't see

- **Can't see:** your message text, file contents, or call media — all end-to-end encrypted.
- **Can't act as you anywhere else:** with the Bastion apps or the web app at app.burakov.net, an organization's server never receives your sign-in. It only gets a short-lived pass that works on that organization's server alone — so even a compromised server can't use your account elsewhere, and signing out cuts it off within minutes. (A web app an organization serves from its own domain is that organization's code, so it routes your sign-in through that organization's server.)
- **Can see:** the minimum needed to route traffic — who is in an organization, and delivery metadata (the "envelope", not the content). Message sizes are padded so even encrypted length reveals little. Identity and licensing are brokered centrally; see [Accounts & privacy](/bastion/accounts-and-privacy) for the full picture of what metadata is visible and how **metadata-minimal mode** narrows it further.

## History that follows you

Bastion deliberately keeps your history available to your new devices and to teammates you add — the way a team chat should work. That's a conscious trade-off in favor of a usable team tool, and it's why the key backup above matters.
