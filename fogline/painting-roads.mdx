## Start and Pause

Painting runs from the moment you tap **Start** on the Map screen until you tap **Pause**. Fogline remembers that
it's on: when you reopen the app, it carries on painting without another tap.

## How a road gets painted

The road network is divided into **stretches of about 40 metres**. As you move, positions from your phone's GPS
are matched to the nearest stretch **within 45 metres**, and that stretch is painted.

- When two positions in a row land on the **same road**, the stretches between them are painted too — so a
    road is filled in continuously even if positions come in a few seconds apart.
- Driving and walking work the same way, in either direction.
- Positions with poor GPS accuracy, and the small jumps GPS makes while you're standing still, are ignored.
- Matching happens on your phone. While the map is open, stretches are painted in as you move. In the
    background, Fogline keeps your recent positions on the phone and paints them in batches — then deletes them.

## What counts as a road

Every kind of mapped road and path counts — streets and highways, but also **footpaths, service roads,
tracks and steps** — except roads that are proposed, under construction, race tracks and bus-only guideways.
That's why your coverage can feel low: the footpaths in a park count as much as the main avenue.

## Good to know

- Fogline paints only where it has **road data** — Kyiv, the Kyiv region and the other regions of Ukraine. See
    [Regions & offline maps](/fogline/offline-maps).
- GPS isn't perfect: drift can occasionally paint a road you only passed near, and riding a train or bus next
    to a road can paint it.
- In a tunnel, the stretches are filled in only if you come out on the same road.
